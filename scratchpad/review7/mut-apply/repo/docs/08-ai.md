# AI (M1)

Goal: an opponent that expands sensibly, defends its capital, researches, and attacks
when it has an edge. It does not need to be clever. It must never stall.

`runAi(state, nationId): Command[]` is called every 5 game days. It is stateless apart
from a small `aiMemory` object kept inside `NationState` (target province, last plan day).

## Priorities per call

1. **Economy**
   - Set tax Normal everywhere; High in the capital if gold < 50 and unrest < 20.
   - Buy PD up to level 5 in every province adjacent to an enemy or independent with
     strength > 20; level 10 in provinces adjacent to an enemy nation.
   - Build a Lab in the second-best province once gold > 500 and a Temple in every
     province with pop > 8000 when gold > 300 (one build at a time).
2. **Recruitment** (capital queue, keep it 1 to 2 items deep)
   - If the number of field commanders < 2 + provinces / 6: recruit a leader.
   - Keep a 2:1 ratio of melee to ranged by troop count; recruit in batches of 10 to 20.
   - Keep mages at 1 per 4 provinces, minimum 2. Recruit the adept tier when affordable.
   - Priests: 1 per 5 provinces.
   - Scouts: 1 at start, replace if lost.
   - Weighted by nation personality (`aggression`, `expansion`, `research`, `pillage`).
3. **Research**: pick the nation color; always on. Mages without armies get order Research.
4. **Expansion**: for each idle field army (commander with ≥ 15 troops), evaluate
   adjacent independent provinces by `score = (pop / 1000 + springs * 3 + wellspring * 10) /
   defenderStrength` and attack the best if `armyPower > 1.5 * defenderStrength`.
   `armyPower` = sum over troops of `(hp + att + def + prot) / 4 * (1 + 0.1 * hasRanged)`.
   Independent strength is known to the AI (no fog for AI in M1).
5. **Defense**: if an enemy army is adjacent to an owned province with power > local
   defense, pull the nearest army back or recruit PD. The capital always keeps a garrison
   of at least 20 troops after month 6.
6. **War**: when `aggression * ownPower > 1.2 * enemyPowerNearby` and a border enemy
   province is reachable, target it; prefer provinces with high pop, Wellsprings, or the
   enemy capital when its garrison is weak. Combine two armies if a single one is short.
7. **Pillage**: Red AI pillages enemy provinces it cannot hold (personality `pillage`).
8. **Rituals**: cast the cheapest useful ritual when mana ≥ 2x its cost: summons when at
   war, Fertile Season / Bounty when at peace, Burn the Fields at an enemy province
   adjacent to a war front.
9. **Wellsprings**: once an owned Wellspring exists and a priest is free, send the priest
   to claim it with an escort.
10. **Pretender**: a fighter chassis joins the main army after month 3; an Oracle
    researches; a Monolith casts rituals.

## Difficulty

- Easy: gold income x0.8, planning every 10 days, no War step until month 12.
- Normal: as above with no modifiers.

## Scripts

AI commanders use default scripts: mages cast their highest castable spell, with damage
spells first; priests Bless; leaders Hold.

## Required behavior tests

- On a Medium map, the AI owns ≥ 8 provinces by month 12 in ≥ 80% of 20 seeds.
- The AI never has a recruitment queue empty for more than 60 consecutive days while gold > 200.
- The AI never leaves its capital with 0 garrison after month 6.

## Implementation decisions

- **The AI contract** (Phase 6): an AI player is `(state, nation) => Command[]`. It reads the
  state, never changes it, and never draws from `state.rng`. Its randomness comes from
  `aiRng(state, nation)` (`src/ai/rng.ts`), seeded from the game's seed, the day, and the
  nation's slot, so a game's command log replays without the AI. `aiMemory` would change only
  through a command; Phase 7 writes none (below).
- **Cadence**: the driver (`src/ai/driver.ts`, docs/06 decisions) calls each AI nation after
  the day's advance on days divisible by its difficulty's planning interval (5 Normal, 10
  Easy), in nation order, and applies the commands in the order returned. A command that an
  earlier one made invalid is refused and logged.
- **The Phase 6 dummy** stood in for the AI until Phase 7, which deleted it (`src/ai/dummy.ts`
  and its tests) and moved its contract tests to `tests/ai/ai.test.ts`, against `runAi`. It set
  research to the nation's color. While the capital queue held fewer than two items it
  recruited one thing a call: a general (its nation's first commander with leadership 40 or
  more) while it had fewer than 2 + provinces / 6, else a batch of 10 of a random troop it
  could afford. It handed the capital's garrison troops to the generals standing there. Then,
  in random order: priests claimed Wellsprings or preached, idle mages researched, armies of
  10 or more troops marched to a random neighbor, and smaller ones went home. Its numbers
  were stand-ins, not game rules. With it, 17 of 20 runner games ended by Wellsprings.
- **Presets**: AI nations, the headless runner, and the New Game default use the nation's
  first preset (Warlord for Cinder Horde, Elder Oak for Thornwood).
- **The AI** (Phase 7): `runAi(state, nation)` in `src/ai/ai.ts` is the AI player of the
  contract above, in the browser loop, the headless runner, and the tests. The numbered
  priorities above are the AI's duties. Each call does them in this order: economy (1),
  recruitment and transfers (2), research (3), priests (9 and the Godless guard), the army
  pass (4 to 7 and the escort of 9), rituals (8), and the pretender's own work (10). Every AI
  number is in `src/content/ai.ts` (`AI`), with a row in `docs/09-balance.md`; `src/ai` holds
  no game-number literals and names no unit or chassis. Spells are named by content id.
- **No fog for the AI**: it reads every province and commander, with no `visionOf` or
  `inSight`.
- **The planning copy**: a call plans on a copy of the state that deep-copies only what
  commands write (provinces, nations, and commanders, through JSON) and shares the rest (the
  map, rng, province effects, battles, messages, and result). `nextId` and `day` carry over,
  so ids made on the copy (recruits, split squads) are the ids the real state makes from the
  same commands in the same order. Each command is applied on the copy as it is issued, so
  later steps see its effect, and only commands the copy accepts are returned. A test checks
  that the real state's hash and `state.rng` are unchanged after every call; a step that wrote
  a shared object would show there.
- **No `aiMemory`**: Phase 7 writes no memory and adds no command for it, so `aiMemory` keeps
  its initial value. The War target is derived each call from the sieges the nation lays and
  its armies' move orders (below). Cadence comes from `state.day`, so `lastPlanDay` is unused.
- **Roles** come from content, never from unit ids. In order: the nation's pretender; the
  garrison holder, which never acts; summoned (a unit with no cost: the Treant and the
  Salamander), which rides with armies and never leads, studies, or prays; priest (a
  recruitable commander whose unit requires a temple; a prophet stays a priest); mage
  (requires a lab; the adept tier also requires the capital); scout (stealthy); leader (no
  pips, holy 0, not stealthy, leadership at least `AI.leaderLeadership`). Every count and every
  step's choice goes by role, so a priest's color pip does not make it a mage, nor a magus's
  holy pip a priest. The docs' "field commanders" are the leaders.
- **The pretender's role comes from its body**: one that may research (the Oracle) studies in
  the capital lab; one that cannot move (the Living Monolith) casts rituals there; a stealthy
  one (the Wild Spirit) stays in the capital, since a hidden commander takes no part in
  battles; any other body is a fighter that stays home until day `AI.pretenderJoinDay` ("after
  month 3") and then rides with the strongest army standing in its province. The pretender
  never leads troops: it is left out of transfers and the leader count.
- **Groups**: an army is a leader with the riders standing idle beside it: summoned commanders
  and the fighter pretender (with the strongest leader in the province) and at most
  `AI.battleMagesPerArmy` battle mage. A move lists the whole group, so it keeps one pace and
  arrives together, and its power counts exactly the commanders in the move. Riders left where
  no leader stands form a lone group, which only walks home.
- **Free field armies** (`eligibleGroups` in `src/ai/army.ts`): a group whose leader stands
  still under idle, pillage, or patrol, leads at least `AI.fieldArmyTroops` troops, is not
  besieging (standing where its nation lays the siege) or escorting (the one group an escorted
  Wellspring keeps, below; another army standing there is free), and has no task yet this
  call. Relief takes groups of any size, and relief and Defense may also take escorts and
  moving groups whose leg does not end among hostiles, re-pathed from the leg's end. Armies combine ("combine two armies") only when they
  stand in the same province, in one move: armies apart would arrive on different days and
  fight alone.
- **The order guard**: no step gives an order or a move to a commander casting a ritual,
  claiming a Wellspring, or building; the plan refuses to issue it. So research never throws
  away mana already paid for a ritual.
- **Power**: a troop's power is the mean of its unit's hp, att, def, and prot, `AI.rangedBonus`
  more for a ranged unit, scaled by the HP it has left. `armyPower` also counts each commander
  as one troop of its unit, since commanders fight; a hidden commander counts nothing. A
  province's defenders are its independents, or its owner's commanders there with the fort's
  garrison troops and the PD force. Walls are never counted. Scores divide by the defenders'
  power or 1, whichever is larger, so an empty province qualifies for any army. "Independent
  with strength > 20" compares the independents' troop count with `AI.independentStrength`.
- **Paths**: every step uses `pathsFrom` (`src/sim/movement.ts`, docs/06 decisions), so a path
  the AI picks is one move validation accepts. The plan searches once per start and pace per
  call. No step has a hop limit: Expansion's "adjacent" reads as anywhere a path reaches, since
  armies refill at home and every target then lies away from them.
- **Economy** (step 1): tax is Normal everywhere and High in the capital while gold is below
  `AI.taxHighBelowGold` and its unrest below `AI.taxHighMaxUnrest`, issued only on change. PD
  and buildings spend only the gold above `AI.goldReserve`, which recruitment keeps. PD rises
  one level per province per call, in budget order: provinces next to another nation up to
  `AI.pd.enemy`, then owned Wellsprings up to `AI.wellspringPd`, then provinces next to an
  independent holding more than `AI.independentStrength` troops up to `AI.pd.border`. The first
  level the gold cannot pay ends the buying, and besieged provinces are passed over. One
  building goes up at a time, and the scout is the only builder, walking to the site; with no
  scout the building waits for the replacement. A Lab goes in the most populous owned province
  but the capital ("the second-best province") once gold is above `AI.labGold` while the
  capital holds the only lab; else a Temple in each owned province of more than `AI.templePop`
  people while gold is above `AI.templeGold`, the most populous first.
- **Recruitment** (step 2): while the nation holds its capital and may recruit there, the
  queue is topped up to `AI.queue.max` items. Each item is the first of: a commander below its
  quota (unless the queue holds one already); the wanted troops; 10 of the cheapest troop, only
  while the queue is below `AI.queue.min`, so the fort always has work; a commander below quota
  after all. Quotas round down and count queued commanders: leaders `AI.baseLeaders` plus one
  per `AI.provincesPerLeader` provinces; mages one per `AI.provincesPerMage`, at least
  `AI.minMages`, the adept tier first while the gold left after it covers the reserve; priests
  one per `AI.provincesPerPriest`; `AI.scouts` scouts. Ranged troops are wanted while melee
  troops outnumber them more than `AI.meleeRangedRatio` to 1, counting every own squad,
  garrisons included, and the queue. The unit is drawn on the AI's stream among those of the
  wanted kind whose batch of `AI.batch.small` the gold pays, weighted 1 + aggression × (its
  gold cost / the nation's dearest troop's) + pillage if it is faster than the nation's
  slowest troop. A batch is `AI.batch.large` when gold and fort materials allow, else
  `AI.batch.small`, never fewer.
- **Personality**: aggression and pillage weight the troop choice; research sets how many mages
  study; expansion discounts distance for Expansion and new War targets; aggression gates new
  War targets; pillage is a besieger's chance to pillage. The leader, mage, and priest counts
  stay at the docs' numbers. This is what "weighted by nation personality" means in M1.
- **The capital reserve** holds from day 0: transfers give leaders only the garrison troops
  above `AI.capitalGarrison`, so the first troops recruited stay home. There is no month
  cut-off and no cheap-troop rule for the garrison. Leaders standing in the capital take them
  up to their leadership, the one leading most first, each a share of every garrison squad so
  the troop mix carries over. A mage or the pretender holding troops (summons) beside its
  nation's garrison holder hands every squad to it, so summons count toward the reserve and
  flow to leaders like recruits, and the mage goes back to research.
- **Research** (step 3): the color is the nation's first color. The oldest ceil(mages ×
  research) mages are researchers: they study in the capital lab (the nearest own lab while the
  capital is lost). The other mages are battle mages, the docs' mages with armies. While a
  battle mage stands in an own lab province it gets carried mana in each color its known
  battle spells use (Warcry, Pyre Storm, Swarm, Regrowth), up to `AI.carriedMana` in all, each
  gift at most half the pool. This runs before the army pass, so a battle mage that leaves in
  the same call carries its mana.
- **Priests** (step 4): Godless danger means no province anchoring the faith (the capital, or a
  temple province the nation owns) holds the nation's own dominion at `AI.godlessAlarm` or
  more. In danger, every priest that is idle or preaching, and the prophet, walks to the
  capital and preaches there, or preaches where it stands while the capital is besieged or
  lost. Otherwise a priest standing in an owned unclaimed Wellspring claims it, free priests
  walk to the nearest one no other priest has, and the rest preach in the capital. The oldest
  priest is named prophet whenever the nation may name one. The AI sets no scripts: the sim's
  default scripts (docs/02 decisions) already do what "Scripts" above asks.
- **The army pass** (step 5) gives each group at most one task a call, in this order: (a)
  relief of own besieged forts; (b) the sieges the nation lays; (c) Wellspring escorts; (d)
  Defense; (e) War; (f) Expansion; (g) the march home. Staying put counts as a task.
- **Relief**: any siege of an own fort is answered, whatever the besiegers' strength, since
  there is no sally and a besieged fort cannot recruit. The nearest group of any size with
  `AI.reliefRatio` times the besiegers' power goes in; a group inside the fort steps out to a
  neighbor free of enemies and back in, in one move. Else groups standing together that are
  strong enough go in as one. Else the groups inside hold, and the others walk to the gather
  point: the fort's free neighbor nearest the strongest group outside, ties by index. From
  there they go in together once they are strong enough. While the capital is besieged, the
  march home goes to the gather point.
- **Sieges**: a siege the nation lays is stormed when the fort has no defenders, or when its
  walls are down and every own commander there, whatever its order, has `AI.stormRatio` times
  the defenders' power. Otherwise it is held. There is no lift rule: a siege that cannot
  progress is held, and War sends more armies to it. Besiegers are held from other steps.
- **Pillage**: in M1 a won battle or an undefended province is captured at once, so the
  "enemy provinces it cannot hold" are the enemy forts the AI besieges. Each besieging leader
  draws once per (leader, fort) pair, on a stream keyed by the game's seed, the nation's slot,
  and the two ids (`keyedRng`), with the nation's `pillage` as the chance. The same pair always
  draws the same, so the chance does not compound over calls. The siege still counts a
  pillaging army, and any later move ends the pillage. The AI pillages nowhere else: at each
  call, before the sieges, an own commander standing under Pillage anywhere but at a siege its
  nation lays (a fort that fell to it, or a siege that ended) is set idle, since an army held
  there by Defense gets no move and would pillage its own province; and besiegers stop
  pillaging when they storm, since a won storm makes the province their own.
- **Escorts** (step 9's "with an escort"): while an own priest walks to or claims an owned
  unclaimed Wellspring, one army stands there: a group already there or marching there, else
  the nearest free army is sent. Every such Wellspring first keeps the group there, and only
  then do the ones left without a group get the nearest free army, so one Wellspring's escort
  is never sent to another. With no free army the priest goes alone. War, Expansion, and the
  march home leave an escort be; relief and Defense may take it.
- **Defense**: the capital first, then owned Wellsprings, then the other owned provinces by
  index (a besieged one is relief's). The threat is the power of other nations' non-hidden
  commanders in or next to the province (`AI.threatHops`). Local defense is the PD force, the
  garrison troops, the own commanders there, and the groups sent there earlier this call.
  While the threat is no greater, the groups there stay as far as they are needed. Otherwise
  the nearest group by path days whose power and the local defense together exceed the threat
  comes: a free army, an escort, or a moving group re-pathed. With none, one PD level is bought
  there. Then in the capital the groups standing there stay, to fight beside its walls and the
  PD ("defends its capital"); in any other province they are free to go, and the march home
  refills them. "A garrison of at least 20 troops after month 6" is the capital reserve above.
- **War** runs from `DIFFICULTIES.warFromMonth`, months counted from 1, so Easy's War begins
  on day 330. Each free army's target is the first of: a siege the nation lays whose
  commanders there and on the way are short of the storm odds (joining needs no odds of its
  own); Wellspring denial (below); an enemy province an own army marches on; and a new target.
  The odds are judged per candidate target: a province may be a new target only while
  aggression × the nation's field power > `AI.warRatio` × the enemy power near it, which is
  the province's fixed defense (its garrison troops and PD) plus other nations' non-hidden
  commanders within `AI.warHops` of it, those standing in it included. A new target is an
  enemy capital the army can take, else the enemy province worth most by Expansion's value,
  discounted by path days, among the provinces that pass. Every target but a siege needs
  `AI.warRatio` times its defenders. The odds are checked only for a new target, so armies on
  their way are not called back when enemy armies move. (Review finding cor-3: Phase 7 first
  read "the enemy power nearby" as one sum over the whole border, every enemy border
  province's garrison and PD plus every other nation's commander within `AI.warHops` of any own
  province. Every AI buys PD 10 next to another nation, so that sum grew with the border while
  field power stays capped by the leader quota, and a large nation's gate never opened: once
  the independents ran out, full armies stood idle in the capital for hundreds of days with
  takeable enemy provinces in reach. An attack meets one province's defense, so the gate is
  judged against that.)
- **Wellspring denial**: when another nation owns at least `wellspringsToWin` Wellsprings
  (claimed or not; a capture clears a claim), each of them is a War target, past the odds gate:
  a claim under way first, then unclaimed, then claimed, then by index. `warFromMonth` still
  applies. An army that reaches none attacks the first hostile province on the shortest
  adjacency route from the nation's land to the first one. Field armies on the move are turned
  to deny too. The nation holds its own Wellsprings through Defense (right after the capital),
  PD, and escorts.
- **Expansion**: candidates are every independent province a group's paths reach. Score =
  (pop / `AI.score.popPer` + springs × `AI.score.spring` + Wellspring ×
  `AI.score.wellspring`) / max(defenders, 1) / (1 + `AI.distancePenalty` × (1 − expansion) ×
  path days). The group attacks the best where its power is more than `AI.expansionRatio`
  times the defenders' and no own move already ends. Groups standing together that none takes
  alone try once more as one.
- **The march home**: every group outside the capital with no task this call walks home,
  whatever it leads, and so do riders left without a leader. In the capital, transfers refill
  the leader, and it leaves again once a task qualifies. A depleted army never stands idle in
  the field for good ("It must never stall"). The leader quota is unchanged.
- **Rituals** (step 6): one ritual at a time for the nation. The casters stand in the capital
  lab: the Living Monolith, then the researchers, the one studying least first, but a
  researcher casts only while another stays studying. At war (another nation's province, or a
  non-hidden commander of another nation, in or next to an own province) the options are the
  summons (Call Fire Sprites, Call of the Wild, Bind Salamander, Awaken Treant) and Burn the
  Fields or Ash Rain on the most populous enemy province next to an own one where no own
  commander stands. At peace: Forge of Cinders and Bounty of the Loam on the capital, where
  fort materials count, and Fertile Season on the most populous own province in reach. Only a
  ritual the caster can begin now (`ritualTargets`) and whose every pool holds
  `AI.ritualCostFactor` times its share is an option; the cheapest goes.
- **Behavior tests** (`tests/ai/behavior.test.ts`) play `runnerSetup(1, 0..19)` (Medium, both
  Normal, default presets) once, to day 720 or the game's end. Provinces are counted on day
  min(360, end): a game that ends before month 12 is counted on its last day, and a nation
  eliminated by then counts 0. "Queue empty for more than 60 days while gold > 200" counts only
  days with the capital held, the nation in the game, none of its own items in the capital
  queue, gold above 200, and recruiting not blocked (a besieged or rebellious capital cannot
  recruit); any other day ends the run. "After month 6" is day 180 on, counting only days when
  the nation holds its capital. A fourth test requires no refused command. A fifth (review
  finding cor-3) plays one Large three-nation game (seed 8: Thornwood, Cinder Horde, and
  Thornwood with their default presets, all Normal) for up to 36 months: no field army stands
  in its capital without a task for more than 120 days while an enemy province its paths reach
  passes the per-target War odds and `AI.warRatio` times its defenders.
- **Tuning**: none was needed. The behavior tests passed on the first run by a wide margin, and
  also from base seeds 101 and 1001, so `AI.distancePenalty` and `AI.goldReserve` keep their
  starting values (docs/09).
- **The runner** (below) plays `runAi` for both nations, with no `--ai` flag, and also prints
  the AI's mean and maximum milliseconds per planning call (PROGRESS has the numbers). No test
  times the AI.
- **The headless runner**: `npm run sim:aivai -- --games N --seed S [--months M]` plays
  Cinder Horde against Thornwood on Medium maps with seeds S to S + N − 1, both Normal AI,
  the nation order alternating by game, up to M months (default 100). It prints each game's
  winner, reason (or timeout), months, and each nation's provinces, population, battles and
  claimed Wellsprings, then the wins per nation (every nation, at 0 if it never won) and the
  games that ended without a winner.
