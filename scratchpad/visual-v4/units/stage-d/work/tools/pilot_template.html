The readability tests of docs/09 cannot run yet. They compare pairs: two spells of different colours in the same family must read apart with the sprites hidden, and two spells of the same colour in different families with the colour removed. Fireball and Entangle differ in both colour and family, so neither pair exists: the tests come with Thorn Spray (a Loam burst, beside Fireball) and Ember Dart (an Ember projectile). What this gallery can show is each spell alone: with the sprites hidden, does the effect's own shape and motion carry it, and with the colour removed, does it still read in grey?

How it is built, and the checks

  
The effects layer is new code beside the mockup's, driven by one data table (45-fx-looks.js, the future content/fx.ts: every timing, count and colour-grammar value, and each spell's fx record). One effects atlas (2048 x 1024); each spell in its own containers, its particles pre-warmed in ParticleContainers (normal for matter, added for Ember only, as the new colour grammar says), pooled sprites and rope meshes for the large shapes; the burst and the vines sorted with the figures; ground rings squashed to the camera's 0.643; light pools in a warm normal blend under an added core; the figures in a spell's light take an added copy of their own painted frame in its colour, up to 0.5 at the peak and only while the light is on them, so nothing glows at rest (the owner's "Glow only during own fire"); pushed shadows; decals in the field under its cap; the docs/12 flash limiter; the OS reduced-motion setting. Above 2x the short form plays without its linger, and at most 3 burst or field effects play at once. No Graphics are redrawn per frame and every effect is a function of story time, so a seek, a replay or a screenshot draws the same frame. The effects sit under the standards and block glyphs, so a numbers layer above them would never be covered.
