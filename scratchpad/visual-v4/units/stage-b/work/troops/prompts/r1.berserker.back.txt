A game sprite sheet: 16 full-length painted figures of one soldier, one figure in each cell of a 4 by 4 grid, on a perfectly flat, uniform pure green (#00FF00) background. The same soldier, the same equipment, the same colours and the same scale in every cell; only the pose changes.

Camera: high above and behind, looking down at about 40 degrees, the way a player looks down at miniatures on a table; orthographic, with no perspective, so every figure is the same size. Every figure faces away from us, toward the upper right of the image: we see its back, its right side, the top of its helmet or hood and the tops of its shoulders; its face is hidden. The figures are foreshortened as seen from above: the top of the head and the shoulders are large, the legs short.

Light: outdoor daylight. A high sun in the upper left of the image lights the tops of helmets and hoods, the shoulders and the upper-left side of every figure with warm highlights; a cool blue-grey sky fill lies in the shadows on the lower-right side. No candlelight, no dramatic dark shadows, nothing glows unless the description below says so. No cast shadow on the background.

Painting style: painterly oil on a wood panel, visible directional brushwork, moderate detail, the same hand as the painted portrait references; a muted earthy palette of charcoal, ash grey, dull iron, bark brown, leather and bone, in which the side's cloth is the one clear hue; soft painterly edges, with small crisp highlights on metal edges and a thin bright rim of light along the upper-left edges of every figure. Each figure has a strong, simple, readable silhouette that still reads when the figure is shrunk to 40 pixels tall. It is not a cartoon: no cel shading, no thick black outlines, no chibi or big-headed proportions, no glossy 3D render, no pixel art.

The soldier: A Berserker: a huge bare-chested ash-grey brute, broader than the other warriors, no shirt and no armour; his chest, belly, shoulders and upper back are daubed with thick dull ember-orange war paint in broad solid smeared patches (paint on his skin, not a garment), with black soot handprints over it; wild matted black hair bound up with dull ember-orange rags; a long ragged dull ember-orange kilt from the waist to the knees, belted with iron; a strip of black bear fur over one shoulder; iron arm rings; a massive two-handed jagged great axe with a crescent blade. No helmet, no shield, no horns. In the brace cell he beats his chest and roars instead.

Side colour: dull ember orange (#C4532C) cloth on the broad war paint on the chest, shoulders and upper back, the hair rags and the long kilt. These are large, clean, unbroken areas that face the sky and the camera, so that a packed block of these soldiers reads as that colour from far away: about a third of what the camera sees of each standing figure is this cloth. The rest of the figure stays muted. The ember-orange cloth is clean and fairly saturated: #D9744A where the sun strikes it, #C4532C in its middle tones and a deep red-orange #7E3118 in the shade, never brown, grey or dusty; it is painted with the same brushwork as the rest.

Facing, again: every figure in every one of the cells faces the same way, away from us toward the upper right, including the figure that is hit, the falling one and the fleeing ones; no face is seen anywhere on this sheet.

Detail: at full size each figure is a finished small painting, not a flat icon: individual iron scales and plates with rusted edges and small bright highlights, rivets, leather straps and buckles, cloth folds and frayed hems, wood grain, worn metal on the weapons; varied, visible brush strokes. The values stay simple enough that the figure reads at 40 pixels: a light top (headgear, shoulders, cloth) against darker legs.

Proportions and scale: realistic adult proportions, the head with its helmet or hood about one sixth of the standing height; weapons at true size. Standing upright, a figure is about 62% of its cell's height; lying figures are as long as the standing ones are tall.
