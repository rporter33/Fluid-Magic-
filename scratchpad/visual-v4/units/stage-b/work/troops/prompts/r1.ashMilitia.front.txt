The figures are foreshortened as seen from above: the top of the head and the shoulders are large, the legs short.

Light: outdoor daylight. Painting style: painterly oil on a wood panel, visible directional brushwork, moderate detail, the same hand as the painted portrait references; a muted earthy palette of charcoal, ash grey, dull iron, bark brown, leather and bone, in which the side's cloth is the one clear hue; soft painterly edges, with small crisp highlights on metal edges and a thin bright rim of light along the upper-left edges of every figure. The soldier: An Ash Militia levy spearman: a quilted smoke-grey padded jack with patches, worn under a sleeveless surcoat of dull ember-orange cloth that covers his whole chest and belly from the collar down to just below the knees, front and back, split at the front below the belt so the legs can stride and held at the waist by a knotted rope belt; padded sleeves and wrapped forearms so that little skin shows; a plain dark iron skullcap wrapped with a dull ember-orange cloth band; a short dull ember-orange hooded shoulder cape (hood down) over both shoulders and the upper back; wrapped legs and soft boots; a long ash-wood spear with a leaf-shaped dark iron head. Side colour: dull ember orange (#C4532C) cloth on the long surcoat, the hooded shoulder cape and the cap band. These are large, clean, unbroken areas that face the sky and the camera, so that a packed block of these soldiers reads as that colour from far away: about a third of what the camera sees of each standing figure is this cloth. The rest of the figure stays muted. The ember-orange cloth is clean and fairly saturated: #D9744A where the sun strikes it, #C4532C in its middle tones and a deep red-orange #7E3118 in the shade, never brown, grey or dusty; it is painted with the same brushwork as the rest.

Facing, again: every figure in every one of the cells faces the same way, toward us and to the lower left, including the figure that is hit and the falling one; the fleeing figures alone turn away.

Detail: at full size each figure is a finished small painting, not a flat icon: individual iron scales and plates with rusted edges and small bright highlights, rivets, leather straps and buckles, cloth folds and frayed hems, wood grain, worn metal on the weapons; varied, visible brush strokes. The values stay simple enough that the figure reads at 40 pixels: a light top (headgear, shoulders, cloth) against darker legs.

Proportions and scale: realistic adult proportions, the head with its helmet or hood about one sixth of the standing height; weapons at true size. Standing upright, a figure is about 62% of its cell's height; lying figures are as long as the standing ones are tall.
