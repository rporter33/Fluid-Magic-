Painting style: painterly oil on a wood panel, visible directional brushwork, moderate detail, the same hand as the painted portrait references; a muted earthy palette of charcoal, ash grey, dull iron, bark brown, leather and bone, in which the side's cloth is the one clear hue; soft painterly edges, with small crisp highlights on metal edges and a thin bright rim of light along the upper-left edges of every figure. The soldier: A Flame Archer: a padded smoke-grey jack, worn under a sleeveless surcoat of dull ember-orange cloth that covers his whole chest and belly from the collar down to just below the knees, front and back, split at the front below the belt so the legs can stride and held at the waist by a belt; leather bracers; a deep dull ember-orange hood worn up with a short hooded cape over the shoulders; a soot-black scarf over the lower face; a short recurve bow of dark horn; a leather quiver on the back with arrows whose heads are wrapped in black pitch-soaked rag; a long knife at the belt for melee. Side colour: dull ember orange (#C4532C) cloth on the raised hood, the hooded shoulder cape and the long surcoat. The ember-orange cloth is clean and fairly saturated: #D9744A where the sun strikes it, #C4532C in its middle tones and a deep red-orange #7E3118 in the shade, never brown, grey or dusty; it is painted with the same brushwork as the rest.

Facing, again: every figure in every one of the cells faces the same way, away from us toward the upper right, including the figure that is hit, the falling one and the fleeing ones; no face is seen anywhere on this sheet.

Detail: at full size each figure is a finished small painting, not a flat icon: individual iron scales and plates with rusted edges and small bright highlights, rivets, leather straps and buckles, cloth folds and frayed hems, wood grain, worn metal on the weapons; varied, visible brush strokes. Proportions and scale: realistic adult proportions, the head with its helmet or hood about one sixth of the standing height; weapons at true size. Standing upright, a figure is about 62% of its cell's height; lying figures are as long as the standing ones are tall.
