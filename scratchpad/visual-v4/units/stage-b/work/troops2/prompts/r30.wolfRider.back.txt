Camera: high above and behind, looking down at about 40 degrees, the way a player looks down at miniatures on a table; orthographic, with no perspective, so every figure is the same size. Every figure faces away from us, toward the upper right of the image: we see the rider's back, his right side, the top of his hood and the tops of his shoulders, and the wolf's back, haunches, tail and the back of its head; no face is seen. The figures are foreshortened as seen from above: the top of the head and the shoulders are large, the legs short.

Light: outdoor daylight. A high sun in the upper left of the image lights the tops of helmets and hoods, the shoulders and the upper-left side of every figure with warm highlights; a cool blue-grey sky fill lies in the shadows on the lower-right side. No candlelight, no dramatic dark shadows, nothing glows unless the description below says so. No cast shadow on the background.

Painting style: painterly oil on a wood panel, visible directional brushwork, moderate detail, the same hand as the painted portrait references; a muted earthy palette of charcoal, ash grey, dull iron, bark brown, leather and bone, in which the side's cloth is the one clear hue; soft painterly edges, with small crisp highlights on metal edges and a thin bright rim of light along the upper-left edges of every figure. Each figure has a strong, simple, readable silhouette that still reads when the figure is shrunk to 40 pixels tall. It is not a cartoon: no cel shading, no thick black outlines, no chibi or big-headed proportions, no glossy 3D render, no pixel art.

The mount: a huge grey timber wolf, its shoulder as high as a man's chest, shaggy grey-brown fur darker along the spine, a large pine-green saddle cloth with a broad pale bone-coloured border that covers its back from the shoulders to the hips and hangs low on both flanks under a small bark saddle, and a pine-green quilted cloth bib over its chest under a narrow bark-plate harness. The green is a clean, clearly coloured, fairly light pine green that catches the sun: #3E9A74 where the sun strikes it, #2F8A68 in its middle tones and #1F6A50 in the shade; never blackish, olive, grey or teal-blue, so the rider reads as his side from far away. The fur is warm grey and brown, never pink or violet.

Side colour: pine green (#2F8A68) cloth on the wolf's saddle cloth and chest cloth, the rider's hood, cloak and tabard, and the spear's pennon. These are large, clean, unbroken areas that face the sky and the camera, so that a pack of these riders reads as that colour from far away: about a third of what the camera sees of each standing figure is this cloth. The rest of the figure stays muted.

Facing, again: every figure in every one of the cells faces the same way, away from us toward the upper right, including the hit figure, the falling one and the riderless wolves; no face of the rider or the wolf is seen anywhere on this sheet.
