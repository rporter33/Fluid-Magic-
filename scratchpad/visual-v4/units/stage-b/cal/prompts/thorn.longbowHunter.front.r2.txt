Copy from it exactly the camera angle, the direction every figure faces, the pose in each cell, the size of the figure in its cell and the grid. Do not copy its smooth grey clay surfaces, its simple shapes or its toy-like proportions.
Image 2 is a painted portrait from the same game, a reference for the painting style and for the nation's materials and colours only: do not paint that person, and do not copy its dark background, its candlelight or its head-and-shoulders framing.
Image 3 is a second painting-style reference from the same set; the same rules apply.

A game sprite sheet: 16 full-length painted figures of one soldier, one figure in each cell of a 4 by 4 grid, on a perfectly flat, uniform pure magenta (#FF00FF) background. The same soldier, the same equipment, the same colours and the same scale in every cell; only the pose changes.

Camera: high above and in front, looking down at about 40 degrees, the way a player looks down at miniatures on a table; orthographic, with no perspective, so every figure is the same size. Every figure faces toward us and toward the lower left of the image: we see its face from above, the top of its helmet or hood, its chest and its left side. The figures are foreshortened as seen from above: the top of the head and the shoulders are large, the legs short.

Light: outdoor daylight. A high sun in the upper left of the image lights the tops of helmets and hoods, the shoulders and the upper-left side of every figure with warm highlights; a cool blue-grey sky fill lies in the shadows on the lower-right side. No candlelight, no dramatic dark shadows, nothing glows unless the description below says so. No cast shadow on the background.

Painting style: painterly oil on a wood panel, visible directional brushwork, moderate detail, the same hand as the painted portrait references; a muted earthy palette of charcoal, ash grey, dull iron, bark brown, leather and bone, in which the side's cloth is the one clear hue; soft painterly edges, with small crisp highlights on metal edges and a thin bright rim of light along the upper-left edges of every figure. Each figure has a strong, simple, readable silhouette that still reads when the figure is shrunk to 40 pixels tall. It is not a cartoon: no cel shading, no thick black outlines, no chibi or big-headed proportions, no glossy 3D render, no pixel art.

The soldier: A Longbow Hunter: a hooded cloak of cloth dyed deep bottle green, with the hood up and its hem cut like leaves, hanging to the knees behind; over the chest a sleeveless surcoat of the same bottle-green cloth that covers his whole chest and belly from the collar to just below the knees, split at the front below the belt so the legs can stride and held at the waist by a dark leather belt; bark-brown leather sleeves and bracers so that little skin shows; bark-brown weathered skin; a very tall yew longbow, taller than the hunter; a quiver of grey-fletched arrows on the back; a hunting knife at the belt (in the windup and strike cells the bow is slung on the back and the knife is in hand). No antlers and no leaf crest: those are a captain's marks, never a soldier's.

Side colour: the hood, the cloak and the long surcoat are the side's cloth, dyed deep bottle green: the colour of jade or of a green glass bottle held to the light, a clear and fairly saturated cool green, a little bluer than grass and much more colourful than the grey, brown and leather of the rest of the kit. These are large, clean, unbroken areas that face the sky and the camera, so that a packed block of these soldiers reads as that green from far away: about a third of what the camera sees of each standing figure is this cloth. Lighter and still clearly green where the sun strikes it, deep bottle green in its middle tones, dark green in the shade. Never leaf-green, yellow-green or olive; never grey, slate, dusty or blackish; never as blue as teal. It is painted with the same brushwork as the rest.

Facing, again: every figure in every one of the cells faces the same way, toward us and to the lower left, including the figure that is hit and the falling one; the fleeing figures alone turn away. In the nocking, draw and loose cells the bow is held out toward the lower left, toward the enemy, and the archer's chest and face still turn toward us; in the cheer the bow is raised overhead.

Detail: at full size each figure is a finished small painting, not a flat icon: the folds of the hood and the leaf-cut hem, the weave of the cloth, leather straps and buckles, the grain of the yew bow, the fletching of the arrows; varied, visible brush strokes. The values stay simple enough that the figure reads at 40 pixels: a light top (hood, shoulders, surcoat) against darker legs.

Proportions and scale: realistic adult proportions, the head with its helmet or hood about one sixth of the standing height; weapons at true size. Standing upright, a figure is about 62% of its cell's height; lying figures are as long as the standing ones are tall.

The cells, left to right, row by row (the poses of the camera and pose guide):
1. (row 1, column 1) standing ready, weight on both feet.
2. (row 1, column 2) marching: left foot forward in a long stride, right foot pushing off behind.
3. (row 1, column 3) marching: right foot forward in a long stride, left foot pushing off behind.
4. (row 1, column 4) nocking an arrow, the bow raised in front.
5. (row 2, column 1) full draw: bow arm straight toward the enemy, string pulled back to the cheek.
6. (row 2, column 2) the loose: the arrow just released, the drawing hand flying back past the ear, the bow arm still extended.
7. (row 2, column 3) attack windup: the weapon drawn far back, body twisted and coiled.
8. (row 2, column 4) the strike: the weapon fully extended into the blow, lunging forward on the front foot.
9. (row 3, column 1) recoiling from a hit: knocked back, head snapped back, arms thrown out, one foot stepping back.
10. (row 3, column 2) falling backward, knees buckling, about halfway to the ground.
11. (row 3, column 3) lying dead on the ground on its back, limbs loose.
12. (row 3, column 4) lying dead on the ground face down, a different sprawl.
13. (row 4, column 1) fleeing in panic away from the enemy: running hard, weapon and shield dropped, looking back over the shoulder.
14. (row 4, column 2) fleeing: the other foot forward, arms pumping, looking back.
15. (row 4, column 3) victory cheer: weapon raised high overhead, other fist raised, shouting.
16. (row 4, column 4) no person: only the longbow and the quiver lying flat on the ground, as dropped.

Background and separation: one flat pure magenta (#FF00FF) colour fills the whole image with no gradient, texture, floor, vignette or shadow; at least 24 pixels of clean background between any two figures and around the edges. No magenta, pink or purple anywhere on the figures.

Never: text, letters, numbers, labels, grid lines, borders, frames, signatures, logos; a floor, ground, scenery or cast shadows; blood; extra people; cropped or overlapping figures.
