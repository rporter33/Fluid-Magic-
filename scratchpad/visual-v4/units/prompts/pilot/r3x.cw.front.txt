Image 1 is the camera and pose guide: grey clay maquettes in exactly this grid, rendered from the game's battle camera; the coloured areas on them mark where the side's cloth goes. Copy from it exactly the camera angle, the direction every figure faces, the pose in each cell, the size of the figure in its cell and the grid. Do not copy its smooth grey clay surfaces, its simple shapes or its toy-like proportions.
Image 2 is a painted portrait from the same game, a reference for the painting style and for the nation's materials and colours only: do not paint that person, and do not copy its dark background, its candlelight or its head-and-shoulders framing.
Image 3 is a second painting-style reference from the same set; the same rules apply.

A game sprite sheet: 16 full-length painted figures of one soldier, one figure in each cell of a 4 by 4 grid, on a perfectly flat, uniform pure green (#00FF00) background. The same soldier, the same equipment, the same colours and the same scale in every cell; only the pose changes.

Camera: high above and in front, looking down at about 40 degrees, the way a player looks down at miniatures on a table; orthographic, with no perspective, so every figure is the same size. Every figure faces toward us and toward the lower left of the image: we see its face from above, the top of its helmet or hood, its chest and its left side. The figures are foreshortened as seen from above: the top of the head and the shoulders are large, the legs short.

Light: outdoor daylight. A high sun in the upper left of the image lights the tops of helmets and hoods, the shoulders and the upper-left side of every figure with warm highlights; a cool blue-grey sky fill lies in the shadows on the lower-right side. No candlelight, no dramatic dark shadows, nothing glows unless the description below says so. No cast shadow on the background.

Painting style: painterly oil on a wood panel, visible directional brushwork, moderate detail, the same hand as the painted portrait references; a muted earthy palette of charcoal, ash grey, dull iron, bark brown, leather and bone, in which the side's cloth is the one clear hue; soft painterly edges, with small crisp highlights on metal edges and a thin bright rim of light along the upper-left edges of every figure. Each figure has a strong, simple, readable silhouette that still reads when the figure is shrunk to 40 pixels tall. It is not a cartoon: no cel shading, no thick black outlines, no chibi or big-headed proportions, no glossy 3D render, no pixel art.

The soldier: A Cinder Warrior: a shirt of blackened iron scales with rusted edges over a charcoal tunic, a rounded dark iron helm with a nasal and no horns (horns are a chieftain's mark, never a soldier's) and a tall stiff dull ember-orange horsehair crest running front to back, arms covered by dark iron scale sleeves and dark leather bracers so that almost no skin shows, a wide, thick dull ember-orange cloth mantle thrown forward over both shoulders like a short cloak, covering the tops of the shoulders and the upper chest, so that from above the shoulders are orange, a dull ember-orange tabard over the scales from the chest to mid-thigh, front and back, a round shield whose whole face is painted solid dull ember orange (#C4532C), with only a small black flame sigil at its centre, a thin dark iron rim and a dark iron boss; the shield face is the largest patch of colour on the figure, a jagged single-bit dark iron axe with a notched edge, ash-grey skin.

Side colour: dull ember orange (#C4532C) cloth on the helmet crest, the shoulder mantle, the tabard and the whole shield face. These are large, clean, unbroken areas that face the sky and the camera, so that a packed block of these soldiers reads as that colour from far away: about a third of what the camera sees of each standing figure is this cloth. The rest of the figure stays muted. The ember-orange cloth is clean and fairly saturated: #D9744A where the sun strikes it, #C4532C in its middle tones and a deep red-orange #7E3118 in the shade, never brown, grey or dusty; it is painted with the same brushwork as the rest.

Facing, again: every figure in every one of the cells faces the same way, toward us and to the lower left, including the figure that is hit and the falling one; the fleeing figures alone turn away.

Detail: at full size each figure is a finished small painting, not a flat icon: individual iron scales with rusted edges and small bright highlights, rivets, leather straps and buckles, cloth folds and frayed hems, wood grain and dents on the shield, worn metal on the axe; varied, visible brush strokes. The values stay simple enough that the figure reads at 40 pixels: a light top (helm, shoulders, cloth) against darker legs.

Proportions and scale: realistic adult proportions, the head with its helmet or hood about one sixth of the standing height; weapons at true size. Standing upright, a figure is about 62% of its cell's height; lying figures are as long as the standing ones are tall.

The cells, left to right, row by row (the poses of the camera and pose guide):
1. (row 1, column 1) standing ready, weight on both feet.
2. (row 1, column 2) standing at ease, weight shifted onto one leg, head turned a little to one side.

4. (row 1, column 4) marching: right foot forward in a long stride, left foot pushing off behind.
5. (row 2, column 1) attack windup: the weapon drawn far back, body twisted and coiled.
6. (row 2, column 2) the strike: the weapon fully extended into the blow, lunging forward on the front foot.
7. (row 2, column 3) follow-through: the weapon swept past the target and low, body turned with the swing.
8. (row 2, column 4) braced: shield raised in front, knees bent, weapon cocked.
9. (row 3, column 1) recoiling from a hit: knocked back, head snapped back, arms thrown out, one foot stepping back.
10. (row 3, column 2) falling backward, knees buckling, about halfway to the ground.
11. (row 3, column 3) lying dead on the ground on its back, limbs loose.
12. (row 3, column 4) lying dead on the ground face down, a different sprawl.
13. (row 4, column 1) fleeing in panic away from the enemy: running hard, weapon and shield dropped, looking back over the shoulder.
14. (row 4, column 2) fleeing: the other foot forward, arms pumping, looking back.
15. (row 4, column 3) victory cheer: weapon raised high overhead, other fist raised, shouting.
16. (row 4, column 4) no person: only the weapon and the shield lying flat on the ground, as dropped.

Weapon motion: chop: windup = axe raised high behind the head, strike = a downward diagonal chop, follow = axe low across the body.

Background and separation: one flat pure green (#00FF00) colour fills the whole image with no gradient, texture, floor, vignette or shadow; at least 24 pixels of clean background between any two figures and around the edges. No green anywhere on the figures: no green cloth, no green glow, no green reflections.

Never: text, letters, numbers, labels, grid lines, borders, frames, signatures, logos; a floor, ground, scenery or cast shadows; blood; extra people; cropped or overlapping figures.
